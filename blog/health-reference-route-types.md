---
title: Give health-reference routes an explicit purpose
date: 2026-10-27
description: A reading-interface checklist that separates topic indexes, calculators, questionnaires and research searches.
---

A resource selector can look simple while sending readers to very different kinds of pages. A topic index helps someone find a subject. A calculator asks for inputs. A questionnaire asks for responses. A research-search guide helps someone construct a query. Keeping these purposes visible makes the navigation easier to understand and review.

This documentation note accompanies the independent reading-route examples in this repository. I maintain [AliGurtuna.com](https://www.aligurtuna.com/); its Turkish pages below are examples from my own project, rather than independent endorsements. Prepared with AI assistance. No personal measurements or questionnaire answers are collected by this note.

## Label the destination before the visit

For each route, keep a short record of its purpose, source and scope:

```text
Route title: the original destination title
Route type: topic index, calculator index, questionnaire index,
            explanatory article or research-search guide
Reader question: the question this visit is intended to clarify
Source: original URL and observation date
Next note: what was found, and what remains unresolved
```

This is an illustrative worksheet, not a clinical record or an implementation of the linked tools. A route can be useful even when the reader's next step is to refine a question.

## Topic discovery and tool discovery have different endpoints

The [health-category directory](https://www.aligurtuna.com/tum-saglik-kategorileri/) provides a subject-based starting point. Use a topic index when you need to locate an area of reading before selecting a particular page. Record the selected subject separately from the individual article eventually opened.

The [health-calculator directory](https://www.aligurtuna.com/hesaplama-programlari/) is a different route type. Before using an individual tool, read its input definitions, units and stated limitations on the original page. A navigation interface should preserve those distinctions instead of presenting every destination as the same kind of answer.

For a software review, ask whether a tool explains a blank input, an unavailable result and an observed zero distinctly. That question evaluates the clarity of the interface. It does not establish the clinical validity of a formula or its applicability to a person.

## Keep awareness resources distinct from clinical conclusions

The [psychological-awareness resource directory](https://www.aligurtuna.com/psikolojik-farkindalik-testleri-online-on-degerlendirme-araclari/) identifies its tools as preliminary awareness resources. A reading log can record the stated purpose of a tool and the questions its explanation raises. Completing a form should not silently change the route's label into a diagnosis.

This distinction also matters for application state. “Opened”, “completed” and “understood” represent different observations. A resource selector can confirm a navigation event; it cannot infer the reader's understanding or personal health status from that event.

## An explanatory article supports a more specific question

The [circumcision-timing article](https://www.aligurtuna.com/sunnet-ne-zaman-yapilmali/) is an example of a focused explanatory destination. Its title describes the topic of the article. It does not tell a navigation component that a procedure is appropriate, or provide enough information to choose timing for an individual child.

In a reading worksheet, record the article title and questions to discuss with an appropriate clinician. Leave a personal clinical recommendation outside the route-selection interface. This note gives no procedure timing, age threshold or treatment instruction.

## Research discovery needs its own label

The [Turkish PubMed and MEDLINE search guide](https://www.aligurtuna.com/pubmed-medline-turkce-arama/) explains a separate task: preparing and following a literature-search route. Keep the query, language and source record visible when making notes. A search result, an abstract and an accessible full text are different reading stages.

For a reproducible reading log, save the query you actually used and the original record you opened. Mark a question unresolved when the relevant material has not been examined. Finding a citation does not establish that its conclusions answer a personal medical question.

## Review the interface with one small scenario

Choose a subject, locate an appropriate route type, and open one original page. Then ask:

1. Did the destination match its label?
2. Were the source and its purpose clear?
3. Were any units, assumptions or limitations easy to locate?
4. Can the log distinguish a visit from completion or understanding?
5. What question remains for further reading or professional discussion?

These checks concern navigation and interpretation boundaries. The repository's synthetic software examples do not validate the linked health tools, provide medical advice or replace individual assessment. Keep private health information out of public issue reports and reading logs.
